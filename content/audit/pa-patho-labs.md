## Unverifiable / judgment calls (left as written)

- **Hyperkalemia ECG thresholds differ slightly between the two modules.** `pa-patho-l08` uses 5.5–6.5 mEq/L for peaked T waves; `pa-labs-l03` says "above 6.0". Both are conventional textbook phrasings of a continuum with wide individual variation; neither is wrong.
- **Sodium correction ceiling is "6–8 mEq/L/24 h" in `pa-patho` and "about 8" in `pa-labs`.** Both fall inside the accepted 6–8 (some sources 8–10 in non-high-risk patients) limit.
- **Troponin detection window**: lesson `pa-patho-l04` says 2–4 h conventional / 1–2 h high-sensitivity; card `c028` compresses this to "1–4 hours". Consistent, just merged.
- **`pa-labs-q107` compensation arithmetic uses a bicarbonate baseline of ~26 rather than 24**, so its predicted values ("about 28" acute, "33–34" chronic) run ~2 mEq/L high. Both are within the normal bicarbonate range and the keyed answer (chronic respiratory acidosis) is unambiguous, so no edit was made; `pa-labs-l07` Case B uses a 24 baseline and is internally consistent on its own terms.
- **`pa-patho-q087`/`pa-labs-q087` febrile neutropenia at an ANC of exactly 500.** The modules define severe neutropenia as "below 500", and the worked case lands on 500 exactly. IDSA's definition includes an ANC expected to fall below 500 within 48 h, which a day-12 chemotherapy patient meets, so the keyed answer is right; the boundary is nonetheless slightly loose.
- **Giant cell arteritis biopsy window** is given as "several days" in `pa-patho.quiz` and "one to two weeks" in `pa-patho-c096`/`q045`. Published yield persists roughly 2–6 weeks after steroids; the two statements are compatible rather than contradictory.
- **PERC criterion 6** is stated as "no surgery or trauma in 4 weeks"; the original rule specifies surgery or trauma *requiring hospitalization*. A standard simplification that makes the rule slightly more conservative.
- **Vancomycin trough 15–20 µg/mL** is taught rather than AUC/MIC-guided dosing, which current IDSA/ASHP guidance prefers for serious MRSA infection. The trough remains the standard exam answer and is correct as a range.
- **Blood volume "70–80 mL/kg"** for a newborn sits at the low end of the commonly quoted 80–90 mL/kg for neonates. Defensible as a general pediatric figure.
- **`pa-labs-s01`** interprets a venous gas (pH 7.52, PCO2 48, HCO3 34) as metabolic alkalosis with appropriate respiratory compensation. Applying the arterial compensation formula to a venous PCO2 is a small simplification, but the venous-to-arterial offset stated elsewhere in the same lesson (4–6 mmHg) does not change the interpretation.

## Verdict

Unusually clean batch. Across 38 lessons, 190 checks, 310 cards, 278 quiz items and 10 scenarios, every reference range, formula, threshold and decision rule checked out, and all ~40 worked calculations were arithmetically correct — including the ones most often botched in AI-written content (anion gaps, A-a gradients, Winter's formula, corrected calcium, ANC, R ratio, CPP). Only two genuine content defects surfaced: one quiz stem that contradicted its own lesson on calcium oxalate crystal morphology, and one anatomically self-contradictory ultrasound stem that made a distractor arguable. The one systemic problem was mechanical rather than medical — `pa-patho.quiz.json` had 91% of its answer keys on a single index, which is now spread evenly.
