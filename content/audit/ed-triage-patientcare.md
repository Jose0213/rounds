## Unverifiable / judgment calls (left as written)

- **Hospital trauma "partial activation" list vs. ACS red criteria.** The module lists amputation proximal to wrist/ankle, crushed or mangled extremity, pelvic fracture, two or more proximal long-bone fractures and paralysis under *partial* activation. In the 2021 field triage guideline these are **red** (highest-risk) criteria. The lesson hedges correctly ("criteria vary by hospital and by state trauma system; these are typical"), and many hospital tier-2 lists do include isolated mangled-extremity injuries, so I left the tiering alone — but it is the one remaining place where the module's second tier sits below the national guideline's top tier.
- **ESI v5 pediatric fever bands.** The module teaches "29–60 days" and "61–90 days" risk-stratified bands and "3 to 36 months" for the immunization/no-source rule. ESI v5 states "1–3 months: consider ESI 2" and "3 months and older: consider ESI 2 or 3" (and adds temperature `< 36 C` as a trigger). The module's version is a defensible, more granular restatement of common protocol, so I did not rewrite it.
- **Bed bath water `105–110 F`.** Fundamentals texts range from 105 to 115 F. The value given is inside the safe range and errs low; left alone.
- **"Hearing is the last sense to go."** Widely taught, supported by some EEG evidence but not a hard fact. Left as written because the practical instruction it supports (assume the patient hears you) is correct regardless.
- **ed-patient-care q024** (128/76 lying → 104/64 standing). The rationale notes that the diastolic drop of 12 mmHg "alone would also meet the 10 mmHg criterion," which reads awkwardly next to a keyed answer of "by the systolic criterion." The distractor it is distinguished from says "by the diastolic criterion **only**," which is false, so one best answer survives. No edit made.
- **Silent aspiration "up to half of stroke patients."** Reported ranges vary widely (roughly 40–70% of dysphagic stroke patients). "Up to half" is conservative and defensible.
