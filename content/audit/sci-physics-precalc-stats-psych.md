## Unverifiable / judgment calls (left as written)

- **Micro prefix written as `mc`** (`sci-physics-l01` table). The SI symbol is the Greek mu; `mc` is the ISMP-recommended medical abbreviation. The table is explicitly headed "the ones you actually use in medicine", so this is defensible, though a physics exam would want the Greek symbol.
- **Latent heat of vaporization `2.26 x 10^6 J/kg`** applied to sweat (`sci-physics-l08`, `c057`). That is the value at 100 C; at body temperature it is about `2.43 x 10^6 J/kg`, which OpenStax uses for sweat problems. The stated value is correct as a property of water and the resulting ~110 mL figure is the right order of magnitude; left alone.
- **Targeted temperature management `32-36 C`** (`sci-physics-l08`, `c061`). This is the AHA 2020 range; later AHA guidance emphasizes a broader 32-37.5 C with active fever prevention. Both are published; the module's range matches the guideline tier this batch is written to.
- **Eye total power `+59 D` with cornea `+43 D`** (`sci-physics-l09`). These are clinical ophthalmology values; OpenStax *College Physics* tabulates about 50 D for the relaxed eye using a different convention. The clinical figures are the more useful ones here.
- **Trauma-triage fall heights (over 20 ft adult, over 10 ft or 2-3 times height pediatric)** (`sci-physics-l02`, `c014`). Matches the CDC/ACS field-triage criteria; some versions use over 10 ft for all ages. Left as the standard teaching.
- **`sci-psych-q001` reports `r = -0.42` between sleep hours and exam scores**, but the distractor it rejects ("less sleep causes lower exam scores") describes a *positive* association. The keyed answer is still uniquely correct and the rationale's substance (no causal claim from correlation) is right, so this was left alone; flipping the sign to `+0.42` would make the distractor cleaner.
- **`sci-stats-q036` rationale phrase "Reporting r itself as 40 percent is the standard error"** reads as "the standard mistake" but collides with the defined term *standard error* used throughout the same module. Wording, not fact, so out of scope.
- **Anorexia nervosa "carries the highest mortality of any psychiatric illness"** (`sci-psych-l10`). This is how OpenStax *Psychology 2e* states it; some newer mortality analyses rank opioid use disorder at or above it. Left as the textbook claim.
- **Autism prevalence `2.8%`** matches CDC ADDM surveillance year 2020 (1 in 36); the 2022 surveillance year reports 1 in 31, about 3.2%. Both are CDC figures; left alone.
- **Scenario `sci-precalc-s02` third side stated as "about `15.1 cm`"** where the exact value is 15.03 cm (the feedback itself hedges "about 15.0 to 15.1"). Rounding, not error.

## Verdict

High quality. Across 156 quiz items, 110 checks, 299 cards, 40 lesson bodies and 8 scenarios, every worked computation recomputed correctly — there was not a single wrong answer key, wrong numeric result, or rationale that contradicted its own key. The ten corrections are four mislabeled distractors in precalculus rationales (choice indices, plus one arithmetically impossible claim), one unit/label slip in physics (`stopping distance` for a quantity in seconds), two terminology fixes with real clinical teeth (T-score versus Z-score for osteoporosis, and the DSM-5-TR panic-attack peak wording in two places), one wrong arithmetic explanation in statistics, and one clinically misleading aside about mean arterial pressure. The physics module was the cleanest of the four; the precalculus module's mathematics was flawless and every one of its errors lived in distractor bookkeeping rather than in the math.
