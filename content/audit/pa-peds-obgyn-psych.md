## Unverifiable / judgment calls (left as written)

- **`pa-peds-geri-l03` keyPoint and card `c016` still say "under 28 days: full workup including LP."** With the tier table corrected, the 22–28-day row (LP strongly considered) is the AAP-accurate nuance while "under 28 days" is the standard exam-level simplification. Both are defensible; the numbers no longer contradict each other on the same page.
- **Card `pa-peds-geri-c001`: "sits alone by about 6 to 9 months"** vs the lesson table's "sits with support 6 mo / sits alone 9 mo." Both fall inside the normal 6–9-month range; the card states the range and the table states the anchors.
- **Currant-jelly stool "in under a quarter of cases."** Reported frequencies range from ~15% to ~50% depending on series and era. The module's directional claim (late and unreliable, do not wait for it) is correct in every source; the precise fraction is soft.
- **Pediatric appendicitis perforation "approaching 80 percent or more under age 3."** Published rates for under-3s run roughly 70–100%. Defensible as written.
- **Delirium in-hospital mortality "20 to 30 percent when missed."** Published hospital mortality with delirium spans 22–76% (Inouye, *NEJM* 2006). The stated band is at the conservative end rather than wrong.
- **Active-phase labor "at least 1 cm per hour in a nullipara."** Contemporary labor curves (Zhang) show slower normal progress; "1 cm/hr" remains the classic Friedman teaching still used on exams.
- **Implant "3 years" and copper IUD "10–12 years."** These are the FDA-labeled durations plus published extended-use evidence; both are commonly taught with longer off-label windows.
- **US MEC category 4 phrased as "current or past VTE."** MEC actually splits history of VTE into higher-risk (4) and lower-risk (3) on anticoagulation status. The unqualified version is the standard board-level simplification and matches how the module frames the rest of the list.
- **Alcohol withdrawal seizures "24–48 hours."** Several texts give 6–48 hours with a 24-hour peak. The table's band is the common teaching version.
- **NJ commitment procedure** (screening service, Screening Certificate) is described at "recognition level" and the module explicitly tells the reader to confirm current state law. Left as scoped.

## Verdict

High quality. Across 161 quiz items, 85 checks, 212 cards, 30 lesson bodies and 6 scenarios, no drug dose, threshold, guideline date or answer key was wrong on the merits. The five corrections are two internal contradictions inside the pediatric module (a hypotension row that disagreed with the module's own card and PALS, and a febrile-infant tier table with overlapping age bands), two rationales in the peds quiz file that contradicted their own stem or lesson, and one psychiatry stem that described a single panic attack while keying and explaining panic disorder. The OB/GYN module contained no corrections at all — doses, screening intervals, and ACOG criteria were accurate throughout.
