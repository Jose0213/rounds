## Unverifiable / judgment calls (left alone)

- **`bls-acls-q0072`, post-ROSC SpO2 92–98%.** The 2025 AHA text targets **90–98%** (and PaO2 60–105 mmHg). The keyed 92–98% sits inside that band and is still the only defensible choice against distractors of 100%, "below 90%" and "any level", so the item was not changed. Worth knowing the wider 2025 range exists.
- **`bls-acls-q0074` duration.** The 2025 Part 11 recommendation is temperature control for **at least 36 hours** in adults who remain unresponsive; the choice still says 24 hours. Changing the range was necessary because 37.5 °C is outside the old one; the 24-hour figure remains a correct *minimum* and was left so the edit stayed minimal. If this bank is revised again, "at least 24 hours" is the item most worth revisiting.
- **`nha-cpt-q0168`, royal blue trace-element tube position.** The keyed answer hedges ("in the position matching its additive, and generally before other additive tubes"). CLSI GP41 places royal blue by its additive; several manufacturers advise drawing trace-element tubes early to avoid metal carryover. Both positions are published; the hedged wording is defensible and was left alone.
- **`nha-cet-q0148`, the 10 mmHg systolic drop during a stress test.** The ACC/AHA criterion is a fall of >10 mmHg **below baseline** despite increasing workload; the stem describes 150 → 132 mmHg during stage 3 without stating the baseline. The correct action (stop and notify) is unambiguous and the other three choices are clearly wrong, so the item stands, but the rationale is looser than the published criterion.
- **`nha-cpt-q0048` (fasting glucose 8–10 h) vs `nha-cpt-q0047` (lipids 9–12 h) vs `nha-cpt-q0187` (GTT 8–12 h).** All three are within commonly published phlebotomy-text ranges but no single authority fixes them; left as written.
- **`nha-cpt-q0149` (glucose falls 5–7% per hour) vs `nha-cpt-q0224` (roughly 10 mg/dL per hour)** and **`nha-cpt-q0220` (3000 rpm for 10 minutes)**. These are the standard textbook figures and are mutually consistent at normal glucose levels, but they are exactly the kind of precise number that varies by source. Both glycolysis items are hedged or consistent enough to leave; the centrifuge item explicitly defers to the manufacturer.
- **`nha-cet-q0090` (limb electrodes on fleshy, non-bony areas) vs `nha-cet-q0176` (telemetry electrodes over flat bony areas away from muscle).** These read as opposites but describe different tasks — a brief resting 12-lead on relaxed limbs versus continuous monitoring on an ambulatory patient — and each matches its own standard teaching. Left alone.
- **`nha-cpt-q0186`/`q0225`, ABG on ice.** Modern plastic syringes analyzed within about 30 minutes do not need chilling and icing them can actually raise measured potassium; the items are written at the traditional NHA level ("often in ice slurry if delayed", which `q0208` states correctly). Not changed.

## Verdict

Strong batch. Five defects in 665 items: one genuinely wrong answer key (the syringe blood-culture order, which also contradicted a correct item elsewhere in the same file), one wrong electrophysiologic mechanism with a wrong key (LL/RL vs RA/RL lead reversal), one item with two defensible answers, one guideline number overtaken by the 2025 AHA update, and one overstated STEMI threshold in a rationale. Doses, intervals, thresholds, order of draw, lead placement and scope-of-practice boundaries were otherwise accurate and internally consistent throughout, and the scope limits for a phlebotomy and EKG technician were handled carefully everywhere they came up.
